I recently went through a "return to reality" that stung a bit, but one that might turn out to be useful.

TLDR: seniority and experience (meaning technical legitimacy) are more or less orthogonal.

I'm realizing this a bit late, after nearly 4 years working in software engineering. Lack of self-awareness or pride? Doesn't really matter.

The intuition is pretty simple: you can spend 10, 15 years in tech, in software engineering, DevOps, etc. If you're constantly doing the same thing, not developing or improving your skills, your understanding of systems, of abstractions, etc.; in short, if you're just showing up without really asking yourself hard questions, then you don't have 10 years of experience. You have 10 years of tenure.

And I think that's especially visible in tech. You're never really done learning. Especially now, with AI, where the ability to produce, to ship, to build has never mattered more. Whether you're at a startup or a large company, the dynamic is the same: a technical profile's relevance isn't measured by how long they've been around, but by what they've built and the problems they've solved.

From that observation, it follows that staying somewhere where you're learning nothing is not just a waste of time — it's outright career sabotage. That can happen, for instance, when a product is already mature, or for organizational reasons where individual scopes are fairly narrow.

This small reflection is pushing me toward more curiosity, more humility, and more effort in how I learn. It's also why this site exists.
